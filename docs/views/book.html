01 / Overview

What we built and why

DocuInsight was designed to flag risky clauses and plain-English obligations before someone signed a contract in DocuSign.

Origin of the idea

In early 2024, co-founder Mehmet signed startup contracts without fully reviewing them. His business partner later used additional equity to force him out, and Mehmet discovered he had signed away important rights. That experience led directly to building DocuInsight.

01 / Capabilities

Five core workflows

Five core workflows shipped in the working prototype to keep contract review inside the signing flow.

  1. 01

    Contract analysis

    Used OpenAI models to read legal documents and pull out key clauses, risks, and commitments in plain English.

  2. 02

    Interactive contract chat

    Let signers ask questions about specific clauses and get answers backed by the document text and live web lookups.

  3. 03

    Legal counsel search

    Generated targeted search queries to match users with relevant attorneys by location and contract type.

  4. 04

    DocuSign integration

    Connected directly to DocuSign so contract notes appeared alongside the signing flow rather than in a separate tool.

  5. 05

    Multiple document formats

    Parsed PDF, DOCX, DOC, and image uploads for analysis across standard agreement formats.

03 / Architecture

Three-part stack

DocuInsight ran as three separate components: a web client, a Python document analyzer, and a hosted Postgres database.

Frontend

Vercel · Next.js 15

Handled DocuSign OAuth, Auth.js sessions, inline PDF viewing, and contract chat UI.

Next.js TypeScript React Tailwind CSS Auth.js

Analyzer

Linode VPS · Python

Extracted text from PDF, DOCX, and image files and ran OpenAI and Groq models for clause and risk summaries.

Python OpenAI API Groq API PyMuPDF python-docx

Database

Supabase · PostgreSQL

Stored user authentication records, completed analysis outputs, and background job status.

PostgreSQL Supabase Auth.js Adapter

05 / Post-mortem

Why we shut it down

An honest look at what broke on the business side even though the software worked.

DocuInsight wasn't killed because the product was broken, it actually worked well. We shut it down because we realized it could not become a venture-scale business. Cutting losses early was the better call so we could put our energy into stronger ideas.

"Too narrow market, platform risk with DocuSign, no clear expansion path. This looks like a vitamin, not a painkiller."

Investor feedback, March 2025
05 / Post-mortem

Four core issues

The structural gaps that kept a working prototype from becoming a standalone company.

  1. 01

    Narrow venture case

    Investors saw a point tool with limited expansion room rather than a defensible standalone platform.

  2. 02

    Wrong market fit

    We built analysis for a step that wasn't a burning pain point. Lawyers wanted drafting tools; SMBs wanted pipeline visibility.

  3. 03

    Platform risk

    Sitting on top of DocuSign's APIs meant a terms change or a native DocuSign feature could wipe out the product overnight.

  4. 04

    No customer urgency

    Nobody said, "We need this tomorrow." Polite interest does not turn into paid seats.

06 / Takeaways

Lessons carried forward

What building, testing, and shutting down DocuInsight taught us before starting the next thing.

  1. 01

    Customer discovery first

    Talk to users before writing code. "That looks useful" is very different from "I need this tomorrow."

  2. 02

    Feature vs. company

    Ask why the platform owner cannot build it themselves. Without a clear answer, you are building a feature.

  3. 03

    Business model first

    Who pays, how much, and why? Those questions need plain answers before spending months on implementation.

  4. 04

    Know when to quit

    Sometimes the best move is to stop early and move on. Sunk cost fallacy is real.
